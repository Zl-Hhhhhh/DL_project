\section{Motivation and Research Questions}
This project studies goal-conditioned preference ranking among edits of the same source. Building on collage reconstruction \citep{lee2023collage} and attribute-based aesthetic assessment \citep{celona2022composition}, I will test \textbf{whether subject correspondence improves global-feature ranking and whether geometric/color features improve label efficiency}.

\section{Proposed Method}
Existing segmentation/rendering will generate eight poster candidates per source, varying position, scale, spacing, and color under a fixed brief. Frozen DINOv2 \citep{oquab2023dinov2} will encode global source/candidate images and neutral-background subject crops. A small MLP will combine embeddings, goal conditions, and position/area/color-contrast features. I will train only this head using pairwise logistic loss and cache encoder features.

\section{Experimental Plan}
I plan 120 new sources, split 80/20/20 for training/validation/testing by source and near-duplicate group, excluding development images. Eight randomized pair judgments per source will record author preferences, ties, and repeated-label consistency.

Shared candidate pools will support random and fixed-rule baselines, a global-feature MLP, and ablations adding subject features, design features, or both. Omitted input blocks will be zeroed in an otherwise identical architecture. Training budgets of 2/4/8 pairs per image will test label efficiency. Metrics include held-out non-tied pairwise agreement, tie rates, blind top-choice comparisons, and source-bootstrap confidence intervals. Three seeds and segmentation/category failure analyses will assess variability.

\section{Feasibility and Expected Outcomes}
Eight of 12 development images passed segmentation/geometric checks, yielding 64 replay-verified candidates; ranking and preference evaluation remain pending. Expected outcomes include representation/label-efficiency analyses, negative findings, and single-annotator limitations. Data, training/ablations, and writing are scheduled for October, November, and early December.
